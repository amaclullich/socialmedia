# G196: Says they can, did it in a test, does it at home

- Category: C20 Ageism, research exclusion, uncertainty and measurement
- Audience: practitioners
- Series: Teaching round
- Post type: teaching
- Visual format: comic strip
- Status: text text_final, visual None
- Working id: C20-P05 (candidate C20-06)

## Insight

What a person says they can do, what they manage in a test and what they actually do at home are three different measurements. US data show discordance between the first and the last, so a function score should say which one it records.

## Angle and position

Anchor measurement in the discharge decision: a score in a letter or a trial report rests on one of three kinds of function, and the reader needs to know which.

Basis: mixed: empirical findings (Glass 1998, qualitative description of discordance with no numbers; Bandeen-Roche 2023, moderate agreement) and clinical judgement (state which kind of function a score records), marked as judgement. The WHO ICF claim was not verified and is not used.

## LinkedIn (1583 characters)

```text
"Can you make a hot drink?" has three possible answers: what the person says they can do, what they manage in a test, and what they actually do at home on an ordinary day.

A 1998 paper in The Gerontologist called these three "tenses" of function: hypothetical (what people say they can do), experimental (what they do in a test) and enacted (what they do at home). It used data from the MacArthur Studies of Successful Aging in the US. These showed "a consistent pattern of discordance" between what people said they could do and what they actually did at home, meaning the two did not match. The abstract gives no numbers, so the size of the gap is not quoted here.

A later US study of 5,888 older adults (Cardiovascular Health Study, 1989 to 1990) found that self-reported difficulty with walking and strength agreed only moderately with measured tests, with agreement scores (kappa) of 0.55 to 0.60, where 1.00 would be perfect agreement. The authors say commonly used self-reported disability items should not be assumed to stand in for the walking and grip tests used to define frailty.

A score that goes into a discharge decision, a care package or a trial outcome records one of the three. Which one it records is a question to ask before relying on it. Asking about an ordinary morning at home is still asking what the person says. Watching what they do at home is a different measurement.

That is a judgement about good practice. We should state in the discharge letter or the trial report which kind of function a score records, and add what the person does day to day.
```

These data show a consistent pattern of discordance between these two tenses." (Abstract)

## Attention and follow potential

Pause: Therapists and discharge teams know the gap between the assessment kitchen and home; the strip makes the distinction visible in a second.

Gain: A vocabulary for saying which kind of function a score measures, and a habit of stating it in letters and reports.

Follow: The account treats measurement as a clinical skill.
