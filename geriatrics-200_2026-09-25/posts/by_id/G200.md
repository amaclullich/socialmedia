## Insight

In a Newcastle study of 85-year-olds, the typical woman had 5 chronic diseases from a list of 18 counted by the researchers and the typical man 4, yet about 3 in 4 rated their health as good, very good or excellent compared with others their age.

## Angle and position

The problem list is what services count; self-rated health is what people report. Keep the comparison literal: 5 and 4 diagnoses, 78 in 100 rating good or better, with the 'compared with others their age' wording and the optimism caveat.

Basis: empirical finding (Newcastle 85+ baseline, cross-sectional) with a practical suggestion to ask the person; the suggestion is advice, not a finding.

```text
In a Newcastle study, the typical 85-year-old woman had 5 chronic diseases from a list of 18 that the researchers counted, and the typical man had 4. The counts drew on GP records and some tests done at a health assessment, for the 729 people with complete data.

Of the 832 who answered the rating question, about 3 in 4 (646) rated their health as good, very good or excellent compared with others their age.

The most common recorded conditions were high blood pressure (over half of people) and osteoarthritis (about half). About 6 in 10 had hearing loss on assessment.

A disease count reflects what was recorded and tested. A rating records how the person sees their own health. The authors point to a study that combined the results of many studies, which found that in people aged 75 or more, comparing yourself with others your age gives more optimistic ratings.

If an older relative has a long list of conditions, you can ask how they would rate their own health.
```

## LinkedIn (1296 characters)

```text
The typical 85-year-old woman in a Newcastle study had 5 chronic diseases from a list of 18 that the researchers counted, and the typical man had 4. The counts drew on GP records and some tests done at a health assessment, for the 729 people with complete data.

About 3 in 4 people rated their health as good, very good or excellent compared with others their age. That was 646 of the 832 who answered.

The Newcastle 85+ Study recruited people born in 1921 who were registered with GPs in Newcastle and North Tyneside, including people living in care homes. The most common recorded conditions were high blood pressure (over half of people) and osteoarthritis (about half). About 6 in 10 had hearing loss on assessment.

A disease count reflects what was recorded and tested. A self-rating records how a person sees their own health. There are two cautions. The question asked people to compare themselves with others of the same age, and answers of that kind may be more optimistic. And this study looked at one point in time. It did not follow people over the years.

A long problem list does not tell you how a person rates their own health. If you are a relative, a carer or a clinician, you can ask the person how they would rate their own health, and listen to the answer before assuming.
```
