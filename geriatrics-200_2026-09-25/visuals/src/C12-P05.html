Incontinence and a care home: five questions to ask

In a US study from the 1990s, people with incontinence recorded by doctors were about twice (women) and three times (men) as likely to move into a nursing home. It shows a link, not that treatment prevents a move.

  1. Has anyone looked for the cause of the leaking?
  2. Has constipation been checked?
  3. Could a medicine, such as a water tablet or a sedative, be adding to it?
  4. Would a commode, a urinal bottle or help at night make nights easier to manage?
  5. Are the right continence products being supplied?
A care home can still be the right choice.
Alasdair MacLullichThom, Age Ageing, 1997; Schlogl, Age Ageing, 2022
