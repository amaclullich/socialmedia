Incontinence and a care home: five questions to ask

In a US study from the 1990s, people with incontinence recorded by doctors were about twice (women) and three times (men) as likely to move into a nursing home. It shows a link, not that treatment prevents a move.

Has anyone looked for the cause of the leaking?
Has constipation been checked?
Could a medicine, such as a water tablet or a sedative, be adding to it?
Would a commode, a urinal bottle or help at night make nights easier to manage?
Are the right continence products being supplied?
A care home can still be the right choice.
Alasdair MacLullich
Thom, Age Ageing, 1997; Schlogl, Age Ageing, 2022
