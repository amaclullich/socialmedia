# Categories for the geriatrics 200 collection

Twenty categories. Allocation is decided after candidate scoring, according to the quality of ideas and evidence, not fixed in advance. Guide range: 6 to 14 posts per category, total 200.

The earlier collection (New X campaign, 6 Sep 2026; digest in `_working/earlier_collection/DIGEST.md`) is heavy on dementia underdiagnosis (28), falls (21), hip fracture (20), osteoporosis (16), pain presenting as behaviour (15), frailty (14), medicines (14), family advocacy (12) and gerotech (12). In those areas, new posts must take a clearly different argument, not restate one. "Pain presenting as behaviour in dementia", "dementia is underdiagnosed", "treat osteoporosis after the first fracture" and "hearing aids and cognition" are already covered there; do not repeat them unless the new post adds a different, specific point.

Delirium is excluded as a topic throughout.

| ID | Category | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Falls and fear of falling | Why older people fall, what a good falls assessment includes, what helps (exercise, medicines review, home hazards, vision, footwear, continence), fear of falling, getting up after a fall, long lies, alarms | Earlier set covered falls heavily: find new angles (e.g. falls as a symptom of something else, long lie, fear of falling as a separate problem, restricting activity to prevent falls) |
| C02 | Mobility, deconditioning and rehabilitation | Loss of strength and function during illness and bed rest, getting up and dressed in hospital, rehabilitation after illness, strength and balance exercise, walking aids, sarcopenia (muscle loss) | Practical and hopeful as well as critical |
| C03 | Hip fracture | Pathway from fracture to recovery, timing of surgery, nerve blocks for pain, early mobilisation, orthogeriatric care, nutrition, rehabilitation, what recovery looks like at 4 and 12 months, the person's goals | Alasdair chairs the Scottish Hip Fracture Audit: never state or imply audit findings or personal views that are not in a published source |
| C04 | Osteoporosis and bone health | Fracture risk, treatment decisions, side effects and their real frequency, drug holidays, vitamin D and calcium evidence, exercise and bone, men and osteoporosis | Earlier set covered the treatment gap; take other angles |
| C05 | Frailty, function and recovery | What frailty is and is not, frailty scales (Clinical Frailty Scale) and their misuse, comprehensive geriatric assessment, function and disability, recovery trajectories, what "baseline" means | Frailty as a label that can shape decisions unfairly |
| C06 | Medicines and prescribing burden | Many medicines (polypharmacy), deprescribing, prescribing cascades, anticholinergic burden, medicines that cause falls, medicine timing and practicalities, pill burden, high-risk medicines, adherence | Earlier set had 14 medicines posts; avoid repeating their specific points |
| C07 | Treatment decisions and proportional care | Time to benefit, screening in later life, blood pressure and cholesterol decisions in older age, surgery decisions, shared decision-making, what "proportional" treatment means, avoiding both abandonment and burdensome treatment | Distinguish a person's worth from the benefit or burden of a treatment |
| C08 | Dementia: living with it and caring well | Care after diagnosis, living well, distressed behaviour (not pain-as-behaviour, already covered), antipsychotic use, activity and occupation, driving, money, dementia in hospital | Exclude delirium; avoid repeating underdiagnosis posts |
| C09 | Communication, capacity and supported decision-making | Mental capacity basics, supported decision-making, advance statements, talking with people with hearing loss or dementia, elderspeak, interpreters, involving families without excluding the person | Address older people directly |
| C10 | Pain | Undertreated pain, assessment, paracetamol, NSAIDs and opioids in older age, persistent pain, non-drug approaches, pain and sleep, pain at the end of life | Earlier set covered pain presenting as behaviour in dementia; take other angles |

| C12 | Continence, bladder and bowels | Incontinence is common but not normal, treatable causes, pads and their costs, catheters, urine dipsticks and overdiagnosis of urine infection, constipation, dignity in toileting | |

| C15 | Hospital care, emergency care and discharge | Emergency department waits and corridor care, harms of hospital stays, discharge planning, delayed discharge, hospital at home, readmission, transfer at night | Services and policy: state facts, not inferred policy positions |
